# Retro — 0.9.7 to 0.10.0 (2026-10-02 to 2026-10-04)

**Scope:** PR #22 (0.9.7: reconnect after a drain, orphan reclaim, keep only
the model and timelapse) and this PR (0.10.0: clear a dirty volume, migration
race).

## What went well

- **Measuring the image instead of trusting the tools.** `fsck.exfat` called
  the stick clean twice — once with 9.2 GB orphaned, once with VolumeDirty set.
  Walking the bitmap and hashing orphaned runs against the ledger proved the
  first; reading two bytes of the boot sector found the second.
- **An independent review before merging 0.9.7** caught a lost timelapse
  wedging its footage in staging forever, and a reclaim that would have
  disconnected the drive every 30 s.
- **Isolating by stopping the loop.** With the drain stopped and the drive
  freshly enumerated, "the printer does not write at all" separated a printer
  that refuses the volume from a USB link the power was disturbing.

## What was hard to understand

- **A fix that changed when the printer reads the stick changed what it
  reads.** For months the printer mounted once and never again, so on-disk
  state like the dirty flag was invisible to it. Making it remount (0.9.7)
  surfaced a flag that had been set for weeks. The 0.9.7 test plan checked
  that the printer remounted and recorded, which it did once — because the
  manual `fsck -y` an hour earlier had cleared the flag as a side effect. The
  next automatic reconnect met a dirty volume.
- **Linux's exFAT dirty-flag rule** (clear only what you set) is not in any
  error message; "Volume was not properly unmounted" on every mount for weeks
  read as noise.

## Follow-ups landed in this milestone

- The printer gets a clean volume after every pass and every boot.
- A dirty stick is reported by `status`, not discovered at the printer.

## Follow-ups blocked (and why)

- **Power.** See the table; hardware.
- **Root cause of the first dirty mark.** Unknowable from what survived the reboot, though it predates it.
